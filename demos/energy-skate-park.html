Can a skater reach a second hill taller than the release point?

Show the answer

Without friction, no. With friction, thermal energy also makes the maximum height lower.

  • Potential energy is largest at track height.
  • Kinetic energy is largest at the lowest point.
  • Friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy.
Common mistake. Speed is not greatest where height is changing fastest; it is greatest at the lowest point.
$$E=K+U+E_\text{thermal},\qquad K=\tfrac12mv^2,\qquad U=mgy$$
  1. Change the skater's starting height.
  2. Turn friction to zero and compare the return height.
  3. Use the thermal graph to track energy loss.
