Why can CO2 absorb infrared but N2 less strongly?

Show the answer

CO2 has infrared-active vibrational modes and changing dipole moments.

  • Absorption requires an allowed transition.
  • UV can excite electrons.
  • IR can excite vibration or rotation.
Common mistake. Every wavelength excites every molecule equally.
$$ΔE=hf,\qquad f=c/λ$$
  1. Compare CO2 and N2 at infrared wavelength.
  2. Try microwave and UV modes.
  3. Identify vibrational versus electronic motion.
