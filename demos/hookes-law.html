Doubling the stretch doubles the force for a Hookean spring. How much does elastic energy change?

Show the answer

Elastic energy becomes four times larger because $U=\frac12kx^2$.

  • The force is proportional to displacement.
  • The restoring force points opposite the displacement.
  • A stiffer spring needs a larger force for the same stretch.
Common mistake. The applied force is not equal to the spring constant alone; displacement matters too.
$$F_s=-kx,\qquad U_s=\tfrac12kx^2,\qquad F_\text{applied}=kx$$

The slope of a force-versus-displacement graph is the spring constant $k$.

  1. Plot $F$ versus $x$ and measure the slope.
  2. Double $k$ while keeping displacement fixed.
  3. Stretch to 0.30 m and calculate the elastic energy.
