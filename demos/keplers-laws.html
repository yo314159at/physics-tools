Does a planet move faster near perihelion or aphelion?

Show the answer

It moves faster at perihelion, because equal areas are swept in equal times and the radius is smaller.

  • Eccentricity changes ellipse shape.
  • Equal times sweep equal areas.
  • The period depends on semimajor axis.
Common mistake. Orbits are ellipses, but most classroom diagrams exaggerate eccentricity.
$$r=\frac{a(1-e^2)}{1+e\cos θ},\qquad T^2\propto a^3,\qquad dA/dt=\text{constant}$$
