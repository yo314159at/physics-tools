If volume decreases at fixed temperature, what happens to pressure?

Show the answer

It rises; Boyle's law gives $PV=\text{constant}$.

  • Pressure comes from particle collisions.
  • At fixed volume, hotter gas has more pressure.
  • At fixed temperature, smaller volume gives more collisions.
Common mistake. Pressure is force spread over area, not a property of one particle.
$$PV=nRT,\qquad P=\frac{Nk_BT}{V}$$
  1. Halve volume at fixed temperature.
  2. Double temperature at fixed volume.
  3. Pump in particles and compare pressure.
