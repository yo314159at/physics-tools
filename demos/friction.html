What happens to the temperature when the rubbing speed doubles?

Show the answer

More mechanical energy is transferred each second, so the temperature rises faster.

  • The surfaces look smooth at first, but their microscopic bumps interlock.
  • Rubbing makes the atoms vibrate with larger amplitude.
  • The macroscopic motion slows as thermal energy grows.
Common mistake. Friction does not destroy energy; it transfers organized motion into disorganized thermal motion.
$$\Delta E_\text{thermal}=W_f,\qquad W_f\approx f_kd,\qquad f_k=\mu_kN$$
