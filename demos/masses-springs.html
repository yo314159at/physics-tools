Would a 1 kg mass oscillate with the same period on the Moon and Earth?

Show the answer

Yes. Gravity changes the equilibrium position, but not the small-swing period $T=2π√(m/k)$.

  • Gravity moves the equilibrium line lower.
  • Damping removes mechanical energy.
  • Spring energy, kinetic energy, and gravitational potential energy exchange.
Common mistake. The period of a mass-spring oscillator does not depend on local gravity.
$$T=2π√(m/k),\qquad x_\text{eq}=mg/k,\qquad m\ddot x=-kx-b\dot x$$
  1. Measure the period and compare it with $2π√(m/k)$.
  2. Switch between Earth and Moon.
  3. Add heavy damping and describe the motion.
